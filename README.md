# LunarBase: Mission Survival

**NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer**
**Repository**: [github.com/thomasteshibel/unarbase-mission-survival](https://github.com/thomasteshibel/unarbase-mission-survival)
**Live Application**: [thomasteshibel.github.io/unarbase-mission-survival/](https://thomasteshibel.github.io/unarbase-mission-survival/)

LunarBase is an interactive, scientifically responsible Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer. The commander manages life-support resources (oxygen, water, food, power), monitors space radiation and crew adaptation, responds to scripted mission emergencies, and explores genuine NASA scientific datasets across space weather, space biology, and physical radiation dosimetry.

## 2. Scientific Data Integrity & Classification

LunarBase adheres strictly to scientific data integrity. All information across the mission trainer is explicitly categorized into distinct, non-overlapping tiers:

| Tier | Category Label | Description | Examples in LunarBase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **REAL NASA DATA** | Actual observations, telemetry, or physical measurements collected by NASA missions, instruments, or ground stations. | **NASA DONKI** solar flare records (GOES satellites), **NASA RadLab** active API telemetry, **NASA LRO Diviner** midnight bolometric surface temperatures. |
| **2** | **RESEARCH EVIDENCE** | Peer-reviewed studies, bio-experiments, and published scientific findings from NASA repositories. | **NASA OSDR & LSDA** flight investigations: liver transcriptomics (**OSD-379**), rodent wound regeneration (**OS-452**), EDL muscle (**OSD-665**), radiation endocrine/immune response (**OSD-566**), plant spaceflight transcriptomics (**OSD-120**). |
| **3** | **LITERATURE BENCHMARK** | Published peer-reviewed scientific literature summaries, flight data compendia, and external international missions used for comparative education. | ISS Columbus Liulin-6R and TEPC baselines, MSL RAD cruise/surface literature summaries (Zeitlin et al., 2013; Hassler et al., 2014), and Chang'e 4 lunar surface measurements (Kiel University / CNSA). |
| **4** | **SIMULATED DATA** | Educational values generated by the local mission model based on documented illustrative engineering assumptions. | Habitat oxygen reserve (20,000 L), cabin control pressure (101.3 kPa), relative radiation risk index (0–100), daily water recovery efficiency (90%). |

### Scientific Integrity Boundaries
1. **Never conflate heliospheric space weather with internal habitat doses**: A Coronal Mass Ejection or solar flare in interplanetary space does NOT dictate internal cabin exposure without modeling regolith mass shielding or dedicated storm shelters (which are educational engineering design concepts).
2. **Never treat research findings as fictional crew medical telemetry**: Published studies describe specific flight cohorts and model organisms; they are not clinical predictions for individual crew members.
3. **No fabricated measurements or silent fallbacks**: When real data is displayed, complete provenance (source URL, retrieval timestamp, collection period, spatial/temporal scope, SI units, and limitations) is presented.
4. **100% Offline Resilience**: If network connectivity is unavailable, the application gracefully renders verified NASA historical records without interrupting mission training.

## 3. NASA Open Data Integration

### A. NASA DONKI — Space Weather Database
- **Portal**: [api.nasa.gov](https://api.nasa.gov/) & [ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/)
- **Integrated Events**: Solar Flares (`/DONKI/FLR`), Coronal Mass Ejections (`/DONKI/CME`), Solar Energetic Particles (`/DONKI/SEP`), and Geomagnetic Storms (`/DONKI/GST`).
- **Features**: Live query controls, custom date range filtering, event type selectors, and authentic offline archives of the historic May 2024 Solar Superstorm (Active Region 13664 X8.7 solar flare, halo CMEs, and G5 geomagnetic storm).
- **Educational Contrast**: Explains why Earth's magnetosphere produces geomagnetic storms and auroras, while the Moon (lacking a global magnetic field) experiences direct solar plasma impacts.

### B. NASA OSDR & LSDA — Space Biology & Flight Experiments
- **Portals**: [osdr.nasa.gov](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/) & [lsda.jsc.nasa.gov](https://lsda.jsc.nasa.gov/)
- **Integrated Research (Independently Verified)**:
  - **OSD-379** (NASA Study Accession): Mouse liver transcriptomics and lipid metabolism shifts under microgravity (RRRM-1).
  - **OS-452** (NASA Experiment Identifier): Weightlessness effects on tissue regeneration, collagen deposition, and wound repair in rats (*Rattus norvegicus*).
  - **OSD-665** (NASA Study Accession): Transcriptional profiling of right extensor digitorum longus (EDL) fast-twitch skeletal muscle from mice flown on RR-23.
  - **OSD-566** (NASA Study Accession): Sexual dimorphism in endocrine and immune stress responses to space ionizing radiation (adrenal gland; direct study record marked unavailable on OSDR portal).
  - **OSD-120** (NASA Study Accession): Genetic dissection of the Arabidopsis spaceflight transcriptome (*Arabidopsis thaliana*).
- **Features**: Interactive keyword search, quick topic filtering, accession numbers and experiment identifiers, mission profiles, organism details, publication citations, and direct links to NASA repository records.
- **Astronaut Relevance**: Explains why resistance exercise regimens, wound care protocols, dynamic circadian lighting, and botanical life support are vital mission countermeasures.

### C. NASA RadLab & Comparative Radiation Dosimetry Benchmarks
- **Official API Endpoint**: `https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/api/` (query syntax: `?format=json`)
- **Native Documented Telemetry Units**: Absorbed dose rate in microgray per hour ($\mu\text{Gy/hour}$) and dose equivalent rate in microsievert per hour ($\mu\text{Sv/hour}$).
- **Calculated 24h Quiet Reference Baselines**: Nominal extrapolations ($\text{rate} \times 24\text{h}$) transparently labeled to distinguish them from cumulative daily observations.
- **Integrated Benchmarks**: ISS Columbus Liulin-6R silicon spectrometer, ISS Service Module TEPC tissue-equivalent proportional counter, MSL RAD during cruise and on Mars surface (Gale Crater, Zeitlin et al. 2013 / Hassler et al. 2014), and Chang'e 4 Lunar Lander LND detector (Kiel University / CNSA; Zhang et al., Science Advances 6, eaaz1334, 2020, DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aaz1334; total absorbed dose rate in silicon: $13.2 \pm 1\ \mu\text{Gy/h}$; GCR charged-particle dose-equivalent rate converted to water: $57.1 \pm 10.6\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$).
- **Benchmark Comparison Scale**: Compares physical dose equivalent rates across Earth sea level ($0.03\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $0.7\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$), commercial jet flight ($3.0\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $72\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$), ISS interior ($\sim 18.0\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $\sim 432\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$), Mars surface ($\sim 26.7\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $\sim 640\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$), unshielded lunar surface ($\sim 57.1\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $\sim 1,370.4\ \mu\text{Sv/day} \approx 1.37\text{ mSv/day}$ 24-hour arithmetic extrapolation; $500.2\text{ mSv/yr}$ continuous-exposure extrapolation, not an observed year), deep space interplanetary cruise ($\sim 76.7\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$, $\sim 1,840\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$), and peak solar particle events ($>250,000\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$).
- **Scientific Unit Rigor**: The unshielded lunar surface does NOT have a 0.3–0.5 mSv/day equivalent dose (which conflates absorbed dose in mGy with equivalent dose in mSv, or applies only to heavily shielded habitats). Chang'e 4 LND is an external international measurement, not NASA RadLab.

### D. NASA LRO Diviner & SVS 5027
- **PDS Archive**: LRO Diviner global high-resolution midnight bolometric temperature browse mosaic (70°S–70°N).
- **NASA SVS 5027**: Lunar South Pole illumination modeling (to 80°S) demonstrating peaks of eternal light (over 80% annual illumination on crater rims) and permanently shadowed regions (PSRs at 27–40 Kelvin) holding volatile water ice.

## 4. Junior Astronaut Field Guides & Educational Experience

The trainer incorporates interactive Junior Astronaut Field Guide modules directly into key mission views:
- **Radiation Monitoring**: Space radiation physics (Galactic Cosmic Rays vs. Solar Particle Events), biological ionization damage, and regolith habitat shielding mechanics.
- **Crew Health & Physiology**: Microgravity deconditioning, bone mineral loss (~1%/month), circadian stabilization under the 29.5-day lunar day/night cycle, and immune defense.
- **Life Support (ECLSS)**: Metabolic oxygen consumption (0.84 kg/person-day), closed-loop water recovery (distillation + catalytic oxidation), and cabin pressure maintenance.
- **Lunar Environment**: Polar grazing sunlight, peaks of eternal light vs. permanently shadowed cold traps, thermal swings (-130 °C to +120 °C), and electrostatic regolith dust management.

## 5. Security & Public Client Architecture

- **Static Deployment**: LunarBase is hosted as a client-side static site on GitHub Pages.
- **API Key Management**: Requests to `api.nasa.gov` use the public `DEMO_KEY` by default. Users can optionally enter a personal NASA API key in Settings.
- **Security Notice**: Browser-side API keys are stored in `localStorage` and sent directly from the browser; they are visible in web developer tools. Users are transparently advised never to enter private or production cloud master keys.
- **CORS Handling & Error Classification**: The resilient API client categorizes network failures, CORS restrictions, HTTP 429 rate limits, and server timeouts, providing informative feedback and automatic fallback to verified offline data.
