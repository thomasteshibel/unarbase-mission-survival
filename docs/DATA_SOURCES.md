# NASA Data Sources and Provenance Register

## 1. Classification Framework

To uphold strict scientific integrity for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, all information displayed in LunarBase is categorized into explicit, transparent data tiers:

1. **REAL NASA DATA**: Actual physical measurements, observations, and telemetry recorded by NASA spacecraft, detectors, and ground stations (e.g. NASA DONKI space weather events, live RadLab API queries, NASA LRO Diviner browse mosaic).
2. **RESEARCH EVIDENCE**: Peer-reviewed studies, bio-experiments, and published scientific findings archived in NASA repositories (e.g. verified NASA OSDR spaceflight biology investigations).
3. **LITERATURE-DERIVED BENCHMARK**: Published peer-reviewed scientific literature summaries, flight data compendia, and external international missions used for comparative environmental education (e.g. ISS Liulin-6R/TEPC baselines, MSL RAD cruise/surface publications, Chang'e 4 lunar surface measurements).
4. **SIMULATED DATA**: Educational values generated by the mission engine using documented illustrative engineering assumptions.

## 2. Integrated NASA Open Datasets & Curated Archives

### A. NASA DONKI Space Weather Database
- **Classification**: REAL NASA DATA — OBSERVATION
- **Official Source**: NASA Community Coordinated Modeling Center (CCMC) & NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC)
- **Portal URL**: [https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/)
- **API Endpoint**: `https://api.nasa.gov/DONKI/` (`FLR`, `CME`, `SEP`, `GST`)
- **Mode**: Live queries via `DEMO_KEY` or user custom key; authentic offline historical fallback archive.
- **Spatial Scope**: Sun-Earth-Moon Heliospheric System (1 AU)
- **Temporal Scope**: Active solar cycles (Solar Cycle 25); curated baseline covers the historic May 2024 Solar Superstorm (2024-05-01 to 2024-05-20)
- **Instruments**:
  - GOES-16 and GOES-18 EXIS / SEISS (Extreme Ultraviolet, X-ray Irradiance, and Space Environment In-Situ Suite)
  - SOHO LASCO C2/C3 Coronagraphs (Coronal Mass Ejections)
  - SDO AIA (Solar Dynamics Observatory Atmospheric Imaging Assembly)
- **Physical Units**:
  - Solar Flare Peak Flux: $\text{W/m}^2$ (GOES X-ray classification: C $\ge 10^{-6}$, M $\ge 10^{-5}$, X $\ge 10^{-4}\text{ W/m}^2$)
  - CME Speed: $\text{km/s}$
  - CME Angular Width: Degrees
  - Geomagnetic Disturbance: $K_p$ index ($0.0$ to $9.0$) and NOAA G-scale (G1 Minor to G5 Extreme)
  - SEP Proton Flux: $\text{pfu}$ ($\text{protons}/(\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s}\cdot\text{sr})$ at $>10\text{ MeV}$)
- **Scientific Meaning & Limitations**:
  - Distinguishes the historic May 14, 2024 X8.7 solar flare (AR 13664) from the May 10–11 G5 geomagnetic storm.
  - **Lunar Contrast**: The Moon has no global magnetic dipole and no atmosphere. There are NO geomagnetic storms on the Moon; solar wind and CME plasma strike the surface directly.
  - **Astronaut Hazard**: Solar Energetic Particles (SEPs) accelerated by flare and CME shocks pose an acute biological hazard to astronauts during EVAs, requiring immediate retreat to regolith berms or dedicated storm shelters.
  - **Model Boundary**: Space weather observations at 1 AU do NOT equal inside-habitat radiation doses without validated shielding transport calculations.

### B. NASA OSDR Spaceflight Biology Repository & LSDA Archive
- **Classification**: RESEARCH EVIDENCE — NASA STUDY & EXPERIMENT RECORDS
- **Official Sources**: NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR: [https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/](https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/)) & NASA Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA: [https://lsda.jsc.nasa.gov/](https://lsda.jsc.nasa.gov/))
- **API Endpoint**: `https://osdr.nasa.gov/osdr/data/search`
- **Mode**: Live search queries with verified offline fallback records.
- **Curated Records (Independently Verified Against NASA Repository Metadata)**:
  - **OSD-379** (NASA Study Accession): *Transcriptional profiling of livers from mice flown on Rodent Research Reference Mission-1 (RRRM-1)*. Organism: *Mus musculus* (BALB/cAnNTac mouse). Tissue: Liver. Status: Verified official study. Analyzes spaceflight hepatic gene regulation, lipid metabolism alterations, xenobiotic detoxification pathways, and age-dependent transcriptomic shifts under microgravity.
  - **OS-452** (NASA Experiment Identifier): *Effect of Weightlessness on Tissue Regeneration in Rodents*. Organism: *Rattus norvegicus* (Rat). Tissue: Cutaneous and connective regenerative tissues. Status: Verified LSDA experiment record (not an OSD study accession). Investigates spaceflight effects on wound repair kinetics, collagen deposition, and growth-factor responses in rats under weightlessness.
  - **OSD-665** (NASA Study Accession): *Transcriptional profiling of right extensor digitorum longus muscle from mice flown on the RR-23 mission.* Organism: *Mus musculus* (Mouse). Tissue: Right extensor digitorum longus (EDL) skeletal muscle. Status: Verified official study. Investigates microgravity-induced transcriptomic alterations specifically in fast-twitch EDL skeletal muscle (ocular vascular hydrodynamics is kept distinct as a separate research question).
  - **OSD-566** (NASA Study Accession): *Sexual Dimorphism during Integrative Endocrine and Immune Responses to Ionizing Radiation in Mice -- Adrenal data*. Organism: *Mus musculus* (Mouse). Tissue: Adrenal gland. Status: **Direct study record marked unavailable on NASA OSDR portal**; official title and payload parameters preserved from NASA catalog archives. Evaluates corticosterone synthesis, steroidogenesis gene regulation, and adrenal-immune axis sexual dimorphism under ionizing radiation.
  - **OSD-120** (NASA Study Accession): *Genetic dissection of the Arabidopsis spaceflight transcriptome: Are some responses dispensable for the physiological adaptation of plants to spaceflight?* Organism: *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Tissue: Whole seedling (roots and rosettes). Status: Verified official study. Explores whether specific spaceflight-induced transcriptional alterations are essential or dispensable for physiological adaptation to orbital microgravity.
- **Astronaut Countermeasure Relevance**:
  - Provides empirical foundational science for astronaut resistance exercise regimens, wound care protocols, immune monitoring, and botanical life support.
- **Scientific Meaning & Limitations**:
  - Studies examine specific model organisms (*Mus musculus*, *Rattus norvegicus*, and *Arabidopsis thaliana*) in low Earth orbit or ground simulations.
  - Research findings describe experimental biological phenomena; they are NEVER treated as real-time physiological telemetry for fictional crew members.

### C. NASA RadLab & Comparative Radiation Dosimetry Benchmarks
- **Official Source**: NASA Open Science Data Repository RadLab Portal, JSC Space Radiation Analysis Group (SRAG), MSL RAD SwRI Team, and Kiel University / CNSA
- **API Endpoint**: `https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/api/` (query syntax: `?format=json`)
- **Native Documented Telemetry Units**:
  - Absorbed Dose Rate: $\mu\text{Gy/hour}$ (microgray per hour)
  - Dose Equivalent Rate: $\mu\text{Sv/hour}$ (microsievert per hour)
- **Integration Method for Displayed Reference Values**:
  - Native fields are preserved in hourly units ($\mu\text{Gy/h}$, $\mu\text{Sv/h}$).
  - Calculated 24-hour quiet reference baselines ($\text{rate} \times 24\text{h}$) are transparently documented as nominal extrapolated baselines, not observed daily cumulative totals. Real orbital rates fluctuate significantly with orbital inclination, altitude, solar cycles, and South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA) crossings.
- **Records & Classification**:
  - **ISS Columbus Module (Liulin-6R Spectrometer)**: *Literature-Derived Educational Benchmark*. Native: $9.14\ \mu\text{Gy/hour}$, $18.03\ \mu\text{Sv/hour}$; 24h quiet reference: $219.4\ \mu\text{Gy/day}$, $432.8\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$.
  - **ISS Service Module (TEPC)**: *Literature-Derived Educational Benchmark*. Native: $10.21\ \mu\text{Gy/hour}$, $21.35\ \mu\text{Sv/hour}$; 24h quiet reference: $245.1\ \mu\text{Gy/day}$, $512.4\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$.
  - **MSL RAD Interplanetary Cruise**: *Published Literature Summary (Zeitlin et al., Science 2013)*. Native: $19.21\ \mu\text{Gy/hour}$, $76.67\ \mu\text{Sv/hour}$; 24h quiet reference: $461.0\ \mu\text{Gy/day}$, $1,840.0\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$.
  - **MSL RAD Mars Surface (Gale Crater)**: *Published Literature Summary (Hassler et al., Science 2014)*. Native: $8.75\ \mu\text{Gy/hour}$, $26.67\ \mu\text{Sv/hour}$; 24h quiet reference: $210.0\ \mu\text{Gy/day}$, $640.0\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$.
  - **Chang'e 4 Lunar Lander Neutrons & Dosimetry (LND)**: *External International Measurement (Kiel University / CNSA; Zhang et al., Science Advances 6, eaaz1334, 2020, DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aaz1334)*. Native: $13.20 \pm 1\ \mu\text{Gy/hour}$ (total absorbed dose rate in silicon), $57.10 \pm 10.6\ \mu\text{Sv/hour}$ (GCR charged-particle dose-equivalent rate converted to water); 24h quiet reference: $316.8\ \mu\text{Gy/day}$ ($\approx 0.317\text{ mGy/day}$), $1,370.4\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$ ($\approx 1.37\text{ mSv/day}$ 24-hour arithmetic extrapolation). The annualized figure ($500.2\text{ mSv/yr}$) is a continuous-exposure arithmetic extrapolation, not an observed annual measurement. NOTE: This is an international measurement from CNSA/Kiel University, not NASA RadLab. The unshielded lunar surface does NOT have a 0.3–0.5 mSv/day equivalent dose (which conflates absorbed dose in mGy with equivalent dose in mSv, or applies only to heavily shielded habitats).
- **Environmental Benchmark Scale**:
  - Earth Sea Level Natural Background: $\sim 0.03\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($0.7\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $0.25\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - Commercial Transatlantic Flight (10 km): $\sim 3.0\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($72\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $26\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - ISS Shielded Interior (LEO): $\sim 18.0\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($432\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $158\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - Mars Surface (Gale Crater): $\sim 26.7\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($640\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $233\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - Unshielded Lunar Surface: $\sim 57.1\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($1,370.4\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $500.2\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - Deep Space Heliospheric Transit: $\sim 76.7\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($1,840\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$, $672\text{ mSv/yr}$)
  - Peak Solar Particle Event (Unshielded): $\sim 10,000\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$ ($>250,000\ \mu\text{Sv/day}$)
- **Scientific Meaning & Limitations**:
  - The LunarBase simulator uses an educational 0–100 relative risk index for operational decisions. Physical detector readings from LEO or Mars cannot be applied to a lunar habitat interior without explicit modeling of lunar regolith bulk density ($\sim 1.5\text{ g/cm}^3$) and radiation transport physics (e.g. HZETRN). Regolith shielding assumptions (e.g. 1.5–2 m berms) are unverified educational engineering design models.

### D. NASA LRO Diviner Planetary Data System (PDS) Archive
- **Classification**: REAL NASA DATA — HISTORICAL OBSERVATION
- **Official Source**: NASA Planetary Data System (PDS) Geosciences Node; Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) Diviner Lunar Radiometer Experiment
- **Product URL**: [PDS browse image](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/urn-nasa-pds-lro_diviner_derived1/browse/ghrm/dghrm_tbol_m_70s70n_jpg.jpg)
- **Product Label**: [PDS metadata record](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lro/urn-nasa-pds-lro_diviner_derived1/browse/ghrm/dghrm_tbol_m_70s70n_jpg.xml)
- **Collection Period**: 2009-07-05 through 2022-08-01
- **Spatial Scope**: Global lunar surface mosaic spanning 70°S to 70°N
- **Limitations**: Archived rendered browse image of surface bolometric temperature at midnight. No pixel values are extracted or treated as quantitative cabin temperatures.

### E. NASA SVS 5027 South-Pole Illumination Visualization
- **Classification**: REAL NASA DATA — SCIENTIFIC VISUALIZATION
- **Official Source**: NASA Goddard Scientific Visualization Studio (SVS Entry 5027)
- **Source URL**: [https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5027](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5027)
- **Model Basis**: LRO Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA) terrain topography and JPL DE421 planetary ephemeris (2025–2028 illumination cycle)
- **Educational Value**: Demonstrates extreme grazing solar angles, crater rim illumination peaks ($>70\text{--}86\%$), and permanently shadowed crater cold traps ($27\text{--}40\text{ K}$).
